# Core Writing

Write in JD's baseline voice: direct, human, casual-professional, and useful. Apply this skill before any format-specific writing guidance.

## Write for the destination

- Identify the audience, purpose, and medium before drafting.
- Lead with what the reader needs. Add background only when it changes understanding.
- Match the expected register without becoming stiff, cute, or performative.
- Use first person and contractions when they sound natural.
- Prefer ordinary, precise words over formal or impressive ones.

## Keep the prose honest

- Preserve the author's meaning, uncertainty, emphasis, and personality.
- State claims at the confidence level the evidence supports.
- Do not invent facts, motives, quotations, examples, or consensus.
- Keep useful nuance. Shorter is not automatically better.

## Cut writing habits that add noise

- Remove filler openings, repeated conclusions, and sentences that restate headings.
- Avoid marketing language, corporate formality, canned enthusiasm, and adjective stacks.
- Do not use em dashes. Rewrite with a period, comma, colon, or parentheses.
- Avoid rhetorical triplets and symmetrical phrasing unless the content genuinely calls for them.
- Do not explain what the reader already knows merely to sound complete.

## Use restrained structure

- Prefer prose for explanation, lists for discrete items, and tables for real comparisons.
- Add headings only when they help the reader scan or return to the text.
- Keep sections proportional to their importance.
- Use emoji and callouts only when they carry meaning or improve navigation.
- Vary sentence length enough to sound natural, but favor clarity over rhythm tricks.

## Edit without flattening

Read the draft aloud when possible. Fix awkward rhythm, vague references, repetition, and unnecessary qualifiers. Keep intentional informality and distinctive phrasing when they work.

## Combine with focused skills

Load the relevant peer skill when the format has its own rules. Use `technical-writing` for technical explanations and documentation, `commit-message-writer` for Git history, and `changelog-writer` for release notes. Prefer one relevant specialist rather than loading every writing skill.
