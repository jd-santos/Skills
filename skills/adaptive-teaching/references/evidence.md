# Evidence and Design Rationale

This reference explains why the skill uses its core teaching loop. It is guidance for judgment, not a claim that every technique works equally across every topic or adult population.

## Strongest practical findings

### Retrieval and active learning

Retrieving an idea, predicting an outcome, or completing a step generally produces more useful learning than another pass through the same explanation. Active learning also improves performance across many instructional settings.

Use one high-information attempt when it earns the interruption. Do not turn every short answer into a quiz.

### Self-explanation

A focused explanation of why a step works can expose misconceptions and improve transfer. Ask for one causal explanation at a point where the answer changes what should be taught next. Repetitive teach-back prompts waste time.

### Worked examples and fading

Novices benefit from concrete, fully worked examples. That support can become redundant for knowledgeable learners, an expertise-reversal effect. Move from worked examples to completion problems and independent variations as performance improves.

### Productive failure

A short prediction or attempt before explanation can prepare learning and reveal the learner's current model. It is conditional, not a default ritual. Avoid prolonged failure, missing-prerequisite guessing, and any mistake with meaningful safety or real-world cost.

### Feedback

Feedback is not automatically helpful. Correct the highest-impact issue, explain its cause, give one next move, and allow a retry. Avoid vague praise, exhaustive correction, or comments about the learner's identity.

### Spacing and interleaving

Spacing supports durable retention, but an effective interval depends on when the knowledge must be retained. Interleaving helps learners distinguish similar cases, but it is not superior for every kind of material. Use both after an initial mental model exists and only when the learning horizon justifies them.

### Multimedia design

Useful multimedia directs attention and integrates complementary representations. Signaling, coherence, contiguity, and segmentation are better-supported rules than adding visuals for engagement. Effects depend on complexity, pacing, learner expertise, and access needs.

Treat the plain-text conceptual path as canonical. A richer medium is an adapter, not the owner of the lesson's meaning.

## Evidence boundaries

- Much of the strongest evidence comes from formal education, including undergraduate settings, rather than workplace learning alone.
- Academic achievement does not guarantee delayed retention, transfer, or independent performance.
- "Adult learner" does not imply one motivation, background, ability, or preferred medium.
- Learning-style matching is not a sound basis for instruction. Treat format choices as preferences, access requirements, or task-specific advantages.
- Generative AI can deliver evidence-based learning operations, but conversational fluency is not evidence that learning occurred.
